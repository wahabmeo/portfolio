The heart is drawn as if it faces you, so the heart's right side is on your left. Red = oxygenated blood, blue = deoxygenated blood.

One heartbeat in three stages

  1. Atria contractBlood is pushed through the open AV valves into the ventricles.
  2. Ventricles contractPressure rises. AV valves are pushed shut ("lub"). Semilunar valves open and blood leaves in the arteries.
  3. Heart relaxesBlood in the arteries pushes the semilunar valves shut ("dub"). The atria refill and blood flows into the ventricles.
AV valves: open Semilunar valves: closed

About 4.9 dm³ of blood pumped per minute

Assumes 70 cm³ of blood per beat from each ventricle. During exercise, muscles respire more: they need more oxygen and glucose, and more carbon dioxide must be removed, so the heart beats faster.

A fatty deposit blocks the coronary artery. Heart muscle beyond it gets less oxygen and glucose, so it cannot respire aerobically enough. The grey muscle cannot contract properly, and the left ventricle pumps less blood. This is coronary heart disease.
